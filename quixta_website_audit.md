## 5. Business model analysis

### 5.1 Overall model

**Analytical interpretation:** Based on its public service offerings,
positioning, and sales journey, Quixta can be understood as a B2B
digital agency with project-based revenue and potential
recurring-revenue services.

It does not appear to be positioned as a self-serve SaaS product
business. Instead, it sells expertise, project delivery, development
capacity, and ongoing digital services to organizations.

### 5.2 Potential revenue streams

  Revenue stream        How it may generate revenue
  --------------------- -----------------------------------------
  Website development   One-time project fees
  Website redesign      Fixed-scope redesign engagements
  Custom software       Larger development contracts
  MVP development       Product development contracts
  SEO and GEO           Potential recurring service engagements
  Maintenance           Ongoing support agreements
  Dedicated teams       Longer-term team engagements
  Team extension        Specialist resource engagements

These are revenue categories inferred from the advertised services. The
inspected pages do not disclose Quixta's actual revenue breakdown,
pricing, margins, average contract value, or proportion of recurring
revenue.

### 5.3 Client journey and sales funnel

A likely client journey based on the public website is:

1.  **Attract potential customers** through search traffic, service and
    industry pages, blogs, portfolio work, and referrals.
2.  **Establish credibility** through project examples, client logos,
    testimonials, company metrics, and third-party review profiles.
3.  **Convert visitors into leads** through calls to action such as
    scheduling a call, requesting a quote, booking a discovery session,
    or contacting the team.
4.  **Qualify the opportunity** by understanding the client's project
    brief, requirements, budget/scope, and timeline.
5.  **Prepare a proposal** with scope, timeline, and fixed-price
    quotation.
6.  **Deliver the project** through design, development, testing, and
    launch.
7.  **Retain and expand the relationship** through maintenance, support,
    SEO, new features, and additional projects.

This funnel is an interpretation of the website's public sales journey,
not an independently verified internal sales process.

### 5.4 Pricing and commercial transparency

The contact page describes a process in which the team reviews a project
brief, responds, discusses requirements, and prepares a scope, timeline,
and fixed-price proposal.

The pages inspected do not provide a complete public price list or
enough information to calculate typical project costs.

## 7. Project delivery process

The website describes processes for website projects and custom
application projects.

### 7.1 Website development workflow

1.  **Onboarding and business analysis** --- Understand the company,
    business goals, customers, and requirements.
2.  **UX research** --- Conduct competitor analysis, user-persona work,
    audience research, and information architecture planning.
3.  **Wireframing** --- Plan page layouts, navigation, and user flows.
4.  **Mockups and prototyping** --- Develop visual directions,
    typography, colors, and interactive prototypes.
5.  **Content integration** --- Integrate copy, media assets, and
    information architecture.
6.  **Development** --- Implement frontend, backend, CMS, and responsive
    behavior.
7.  **Testing and launch** --- Perform quality assurance, performance
    checks, SEO checks, security checks, and deployment.
8.  **Post-launch maintenance** --- Provide monitoring, updates,

## 14. Completeness and limitations of this document

The prior inspection covered the homepage, principal service pages,
portfolio pages, About, Careers, Contact, FAQs, technology stack,
testimonials, AI information, and three industry pages.

This document consolidates those findings. It is **not** a literal
full-text mirror and does not claim to contain every word, every page,
or every dynamically loaded element on Quixta's website.

For a genuinely exhaustive technical crawl, the remaining work would
include:

-   Every individual portfolio case study
-   Every blog article and its metadata
-   All industry landing pages
-   Every FAQ entry
-   All nested navigation and footer links
-   Contact form fields and public business details
-   Publicly accessible sitemap and robots directives
-   Page titles and meta descriptions
-   Complete heading hierarchy (H1--H6)
-   Dynamically loaded content and relevant page interactions

A robust full crawl should store one record per URL, including page
title, meta description, headings, body copy, CTAs, service categories,
target audience, internal links, and source URL.
